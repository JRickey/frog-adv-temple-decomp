    - target: `subs r0, r1, r0`  (result in op1's / y's register r0)
    - built:  `subs r1, r1, r0`  (result in op0's / field_36's register r1,
              then `asrs r0, r1, #16` moves it to r0 two insns later)
  The two `ldrh` loads and their registers are CORRECT under the pins
  (field_36→r1 first, y→r0 second). Only the SUB output register diverges.
- **best CLEAN byte_diff: 4** (diff_count 2), NO pins, per-TU
  `CFLAGS += -fno-gcse -fno-expensive-optimizations`. Here the registers are
  right (`subs r0, r1, r0`) but the two `ldrh` value-loads emit in SWAPPED
  ORDER (built loads y first, target loads field_36 first). This is the
  cleanest readable form (the "## Best-effort C" below) and is preferred over
  the pinned byte-3 form for readability.

The two near-matches are the SAME underlying agbcc allocation conflict from
two angles:
  - WITHOUT pins: `-fno-expensive-optimizations` fixes the subtract-output
    register (r0) but reverses the two value-load emission order.
  - WITH r0/r1 pins: load order + both operand regs become correct, but the
    non-commutative SUB reuses op0's dead register (r1) for its output instead
    of op1's dead register (r0). agbcc's `subsi3` reload prefers the first
    operand's register for the result; the baserom got the second operand's.
