## Drift (best byte_diff 135, diff_count 23)

Every remaining diff is ONE register-allocation decision + its address-shift
cascade — NOT a logic error:

- agbcc keeps the loop-invariant `opcode` VALUE cached in `ip` across the loop
  (`ldrb r1,[r6,#26]; mov ip,r1` in the preheader) and indexes the entity array
  through r6 directly (`adds r2, r6, r0`).
- The baserom instead caches the header BASE in `ip` (`mov ip, r6`), indexes
  through the ip copy (`mov r1, ip; adds r2, r0, r1`), and **re-reads** the
  opcode fresh after the loop (`ldrb r0, [r6, #26]` at the second switch).
- Also a minor load-scheduling diff at the loop head: target loads gIwram_3720
  into a temp then `adds r6, r0, #0`; agbcc loads straight into r6.

This is the narrow Class-1 "high-reg holding loop-state across the loop body"
case: a base copy held in `ip` across the scan while a header field is re-read
post-loop. agbcc has a free register (ip) in my structure and spends it caching
the opcode rather than copying the base.
